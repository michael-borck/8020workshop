---
title: "Winter is telescope weather"
date: 2026-05-09
tags: ["astronomy", "80-20"]
---

Perth summer is hard on astronomy. The nights are short, the air shimmers over hot roofs, and by the time it is properly dark I am thinking about bed. Then May arrives, the evenings cool down, and the whole hobby changes.

Winter puts the centre of the Milky Way high in the evening sky. That is the good part of living in the southern hemisphere and it costs nothing. No new eyepiece gives you that. You just have to be outside when it happens.

The cold sorts out your gear fast. Dew forms on anything left uncovered. Batteries give up early. Fingers stop cooperating with small screws. I have learned to set up the fiddly parts before dark, keep the routine simple, and wear more layers than feels reasonable at sunset. A thermos does more for a winter session than most accessories in the catalogue.

This is where the cheap scopes earn their keep. A small scope on a simple mount gets used on a Tuesday night because it takes five minutes to carry out. The big impressive setup stays inside unless the night looks perfect, and perfect nights are rare enough that it can go a month between outings. Most weeks the small scope is the one that goes out.

Winter also forgives a suburban backyard. Neighbours go inside early. Lights go off. The sky gets a little darker for free.

My plan for the cold months is modest: one clear night a week, one scope at a time, a hot drink, a red torch, and no agenda longer than a sticky note.

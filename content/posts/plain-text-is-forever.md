Your apps will die. Your files shouldn't.

Every few years the productivity world reinvents itself. A new app arrives with slick animations, nice themes and a subscription model. You migrate everything over, learn the shortcuts, build your system. Then the company pivots, gets acquired or stops updating. You are left exporting whatever you can salvage into plain text.

So start there.

This is a short series about the most boring, most durable 80-20 technology choice you can make: storing your work in plain text.

## The series

- [The Bet That Always Pays Off](/posts/plain-text-the-bet/). A `.txt` file from 1985 still opens instantly. A `.doc` from 2005 might not. Plain text is the safest long-term bet you can make.

- [Tools Come and Go, Text Stays](/posts/plain-text-tools-come-and-go/). Obsidian, Logseq, Quarto, Vim, VS Code: they all read the same files. The difference between choosing a tool and choosing a format.

- [Markdown: Plain Text With Just Enough Structure](/posts/plain-text-markdown/). When raw `.txt` isn't quite enough, Markdown gives you headings, links and code blocks without leaving the keyboard. Write once, render everywhere.

- [The 80/20 of Productivity Tools](/posts/plain-text-8020-productivity/). A text editor, a folder structure and the discipline to write things down. That is the whole system.

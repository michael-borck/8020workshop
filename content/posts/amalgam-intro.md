A precision 3D printer built from any two dead donor printers, for under $300 AUD. That is the premise of The Amalgam, a parametric design that adapts to whatever salvaged parts you feed it.

Three variants so far. Scaffold uses threaded rods from Bunnings, Mill uses scavenged extrusions, and Lathe uses smooth rods for precision.

The design lives in build123d with YAML-driven parametric generation. Feed it your donor specs and you get custom STLs and a personalised assembly manual.

Early days, but the M10 threaded rod frame prototype is surprisingly rigid.

Same philosophy as the printers and telescopes: every project has a job, nothing exists without a purpose, and the 80-20 principle runs through it.

### Retroverse Studios: indie games

Retroverse Studios is my indie game label, focused on retro-style and pixel-art games. Current projects:

- **Reality Reigns.** A swipe-based decision game inspired by *Reigns*, but data-driven and configurable. Swap the theme file and it becomes space, fantasy, cyberpunk or an educational scenario. The engine stays the same and the content is modular.
- **Atari 2600 Tribute Series.** Four games paying homage to the Atari 2600 era, including a clone of *Simon Was Alone*. Built as a love letter to the hardware that started it all.
- **LanePlay.** A tenpin bowling simulator where you enter your real scores and compete against current PBA players. Their stats drive the simulated performances, so you play against realistic competition.

The common thread is small scope, clear mechanics and systems that punch above their weight through smart design rather than a big budget.

### EdTech tools: a teaching suite

I build software tools for education. Instead of one monolithic platform, each tool solves a specific problem:

**Curriculum Creator**, **Feed Forward**, **Talk Buddy**, **Study Buddy**, **Career Compass**, **Cite Sight**, **The AI Exchanger** and **The AI Skills Passport**.

They share a philosophy: give educators and students focused tools that do one thing well, without feature bloat, enterprise pricing or a six-month onboarding process. The 80-20 principle applied to educational software, keeping the features that matter and dropping the ones that don't.

### Open source: libraries, packages and containers

Python libraries on PyPI, npm packages and Docker Hub containers. Some support the edtech tools directly, with shared utilities, deployment scaffolding and common patterns extracted into reusable packages. Others are standalone projects built for the community.

The goal is the same: solve a specific problem, document it clearly and ship it.

### The 80-20 take

Software is where the 80-20 principle applies to both money and scope. Every project starts with the same question: what is the smallest thing I can build that delivers the most value? A data-driven game engine that powers dozens of themes. A teaching tool that does one job without a settings page longer than the feature list. A library that saves ten minutes on every project for the next five years.

Build small, ship often, and let the work compound.

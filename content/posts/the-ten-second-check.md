title: "The ten second check"
date: 2026-06-14

I have spent part of this year writing a book about using AI at work. The working title is The Human Edge, and the short version of it fits in one line. Everyone has the same AI tools now, so the edge is judgement.

That sounds like work talk. It is, mostly. But I keep catching the same habit at the bench and in the backyard, and it was there long before the book.

The habit takes about ten seconds. Before I act on something, I ask how much I should trust it and what a quick check would look like. With AI output the check might be opening the source data instead of forwarding a tidy summary. With a Marketplace listing it is asking for a photo of the model plate instead of trusting the headline. With a telescope it is a look through the eyepiece before I blame the collimation. With a printer it is checking the belt before I rebuild the firmware config.

The mistakes I regret usually share one step. I skipped the check because the first answer looked complete. A polished AI report, a confident seller, a clean first layer. Fluency feels like evidence. It is not.

I am not going to turn this site into a series of lessons from the book. Wrong room for that. The book can live with the work stuff. I am writing this down here because the workshop taught me the habit first and the writing just gave it a name.

Trust slowly. Check cheaply. The cheap check is usually the 20 percent that saves the other 80.

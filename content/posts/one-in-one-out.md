title: "One in, one out"
date: 2026-10-10

October is when I look around the workshop and count. Printers on the bench, printers under it, telescopes in cases, boxes of parts I swore were sorted. Some of it earns its place every week. Some of it is just there because it arrived during a burst of enthusiasm and never left.

So here is the spring rule. One in, one out. If a new project comes home from Marketplace, something of the same size leaves. Sold, gifted, or stripped for parts and the frame gone. No exceptions for bargains, because bargains are exactly how the shelves filled up in the first place.

This is the other side of the deal fever post from February. Finding a good deal is a skill. Walking past one is harder, and it took me longer to learn.

The test I use now is simple. Has this thing been used, worked on, or planned in the last three months. If the answer is no, call it storage. Storage is fine for parts. It is a sad job for a whole machine that somebody else would enjoy.

Letting things go has a side effect I did not expect. The remaining gear gets more use. Fewer choices at setup time, fewer half finished jobs competing for the same Saturday. The fleet idea only works if every machine has a job. A machine with no job is just a queue.

The workshop feels better a little emptier, and so does the head that works in it.

Yes, I have a lot of printers. No, I don't need an intervention.

Every machine was picked up strategically, most for well under the cost of a single new printer cartridge. I built a parallel-processing workshop from other people's "obsolete" cast-offs, and each machine has a defined role or is a learning project in its own right.

Most people with one printer talk about getting a second. I have a fleet that covers every material, speed bracket and geometric challenge, and I spent less in total than someone dropping money on a single flagship combo.

### The value is in the project builds

You can buy a printer, unbox it, run the wizard and hit print. You will get great results and learn almost nothing about how it works.

Building a Mendel i2 from M8 threaded rod teaches frame rigidity and motion systems. Restoring a LulzBot AO-101 with a seesaw bed mod teaches mechanical levelling and thermal management. Modding a MakerFarm i3v from wood to printed parts and 2020 extrusion teaches iterative design. Designing The Amalgam from scratch teaches all of it at once.

Those machines teach me things a plug-and-play printer never will.

### The curriculum

The workhorses handle production. The specialists handle edge cases. The project builds handle education. Together they give a complete understanding of how 3D printing works, from firmware to filament path to frame dynamics.

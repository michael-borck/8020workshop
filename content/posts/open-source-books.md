title: "What writing seven books taught me"
date: 2026-03-04
tags: ["coding", "80-20", "thinking"]
---

I wrote seven books over the past few years. I didn't set out to be an author. I had opinions about how we teach programming and use AI, and writing them down was the honest way to test whether they held up.

All seven are open source on GitHub, built with Quarto and published free on KDP. The AI titles are *Conversation, Not Delegation* and *Partner, Don't Police*. The Python ones are *Think Python, Direct AI*, *Code Python, Consult AI* and *Ship Python, Orchestrate AI*. Then *Build Web, Guide AI*, and *Book Forge*, which is a tool rather than a book.

The publishing turned out to be the easy part. Quarto handles the formats, GitHub holds the source and KDP takes the file. The writing is what takes the time. For years I assumed a book needed a publisher, an agent and a lot of waiting. It needed a folder, a build command and something worth saying.

Anyone can read them, fork them or tell me where I am wrong. That is the main reason they are open source. An idea is easier to test when other people can check it.
